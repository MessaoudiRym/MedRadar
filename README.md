# MedRadar

**MedRadar helps Algerian patients find an out-of-stock medicine: a registered equivalent, or a nearby pharmacy that has it.**

## The problem

Medicine shortages are frequent in Algeria. Patients, especially those with chronic conditions (diabetes, hypertension, thyroid disorders, epilepsy), go from pharmacy to pharmacy or back to their doctor, even when:

- another medicine with the **same active ingredient, dose and form** is registered and available, or
- a pharmacy two kilometres away has it in stock.

## What MedRadar does

One question: *"I can't find my medicine, what now?"* Two answers:

1. **Equivalents** — medicines with the same active ingredient (INN/DCI), the same dose and the same form, taken from the official Algerian national nomenclature. Equivalents are found by fixed rules on official data, never generated by AI.
2. **Availability** — a map of nearby pharmacies that declared the medicine in stock, with an alert when it comes back.

Pharmacists update their stock in two taps.

> MedRadar never replaces a pharmacist's or doctor's advice. It shows options to discuss with them.

## What is real and what is simulated

| Data | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Medicines and equivalents | **Real** — national nomenclature of pharmaceutical products, Ministry of Pharmaceutical Industry ([miph.gov.dz](https://www.miph.gov.dz)). Version: August 2026 |
| Pharmacies and stock levels | **Simulated** for the demo — fictional pharmacies placed in real Oran neighbourhoods, until real pharmacies join |

**1. Create the `.env` file** at the project root, then replace every value (never commit it):

```bash

```

## Pharmacist accounts

1. **Registration.** A pharmacist creates an account and their pharmacy in one step (`POST /auth/register-pharmacist`). The pharmacy starts as *pending*: it is invisible to patients and cannot declare stock.
2. **Verification.** An admin checks the pharmacy (professional number, address, phone) and approves, rejects or suspends it, with a reason for a rejection or suspension. Changing the address or position later sends the pharmacy back to verification.
3. **Declaration.** Once approved, the pharmacist marks medicines "in stock" or "out of stock" for their own pharmacy only, one by one or up to 50 at a time. Patients see the latest declaration; one older than 7 days is shown as "to be confirmed by phone".

Admin accounts are only created by `scripts/create_admin.py`; no API route can create one. See [docs/privacy.md](docs/privacy.md) for the data stored.

**Demo data.** The 40 demo pharmacies, their stock levels and the demo pharmacist accounts are simulated and fictional: names, addresses and phone numbers (0550 00 00 XX) are made up, and positions are random points in real Oran districts. API responses say so (`"demo_data": true`). Only the medicines and their equivalents come from official data.

Demo logins, on http://localhost:3000/pharmacien/connexion, all with the password `DEMO_PHARMACIST_PASSWORD` from `.env`:

| Account | Pharmacy |
| --- | --- |
| `pharmacien1@medradar.demo` | Approved, with 4 delivery notes in its history |
| `pharmacien2@medradar.demo` | Approved |
| `pharmacien3@medradar.demo` | Waiting for verification (cannot declare stock) |

The admin account is the one of `ADMIN_EMAIL` and `ADMIN_PASSWORD`.

## How equivalents are found

Two medicines are suggested as equivalents only when all three rules match:

1. **Same active ingredient.** The INN/DCI is cleaned so that different spellings of the same substance match: "CHLORHYDRATE DE METFORMINE" = "METFORMINE CHLORHYDRATE", "AMLODIPINE BESILATE EXPRIME EN AMLODIPINE" = "AMLODIPINE SOUS FORME BESYLATE", and "IRBESARTAN/AMLODIPINE" = "AMLODIPINE/IRBESARTAN".
2. **Same dose, after normalization.** "1G", "1 000 MG" and "1000MG" are the same dose; micrograms, grams and litres are converted to one unit.
3. **Same form group.** The ~680 ways the file writes forms are grouped into about thirty groups (tablet, capsule, syrup, injectable, eye drops...). Modified-release and gastro-resistant forms are always kept apart: an extended-release tablet is never suggested in place of a normal tablet.

Only medicines sold in community pharmacies and not withdrawn or non-renewed are suggested.

## Multilingual search

Many Algerian patients write the name of their medicine in Arabic letters ("غلوكوفاج"), in Darija ("dwa ta3 glucophage", "glucofaj 850") or know it under its English or foreign name ("metformin", "Tylenol"). The search understands all of them, with one rule: **the language model understands and translates names, the database checks, the patient confirms.**

1. **Without AI, first.** The query is cleaned (accents, Arabic digits "٨٥٠" → 850, linking words such as "dwa", "ta3", "دوا", "medicine" removed), its language is detected from its script and common words, and a written dose is kept. Names in Arabic letters or arabizi are found by **transliteration rules**: every name of the nomenclature and the query are written as the same "sound key" (ف = F/PH/V, ك = K/C, ج = J/G...), so "غلوكوفاج" and "glucofaj" both find GLUCOPHAGE in about a millisecond.
2. **The model, only when it helps.** Suggestions shown while typing never call the model (`interpret=false`). When the patient validates the search (Enter or the Search button, `interpret=true`), Google Gemini (Flash, free tier) may be asked what *name* was typed: a brand, a molecule (metformin → METFORMINE, acetaminophen → PARACETAMOL), or a foreign brand and its molecule. With `ARABIC_SEARCH_MODE=llm_first` (default) it is asked for Arabic, Darija, arabizi and English queries, and for French queries the normal search is unsure about; with `rules_first`, only when the rules are unsure.
3. **The database checks.** Every name proposed by the model must sound like what the patient typed, and must exist in the nomenclature (sold in pharmacies, not withdrawn); a dose must be written in the query. Nothing else is ever shown. The results page says what was understood ("You wrote “غلوكوفاج” — we understood: GLUCOPHAGE") and marks uncertain answers *to check*.

**What it never does: go from a disease or a symptom to a medicine.** "دوا السكر", "medicine for diabetes" or "dwa ta3 rass" give no result and a message asking for the name on the box or advice from a pharmacist. This is guaranteed by our code, not by the model: a list of disease, symptom and use words in French, Arabic, Darija and English is checked before any call, and a model answer "disease or symptom" also gives no result, even if it proposes a medicine.

**Without AI, it still works.** Without a key, or when the model is slow (8 s), over quota or wrong, the search silently uses the rules: the patient never sees an error.

**Saving the free quota.** No call while typing; a 30-day cache in the database (`interpretations_cache`), so the same query is never sent twice; at most 20 calls per hour per IP (cached answers do not count). Only the text of the query is sent, and logs never contain it (see [docs/privacy.md](docs/privacy.md)).

**Results** (69 test queries, October 2026, real Gemini free tier): the right medicine comes first for **100 %** of the queries in both modes (brands and molecules in Arabic letters, Darija, arabizi, English, foreign brands, French typos), and **100 % of the 16 disease and symptom queries are refused**. `llm_first` made 41 calls to the model (0.8 s per search on average), `rules_first` only 12 (0.2 s). Without any model, the rules alone already reach 90 %: the failures are translations such as acetaminophen → PARACETAMOL and foreign brands. These queries were written for the project and used to fix the failures they revealed, so they should be completed with queries typed by real patients. See [docs/multilingual_results.md](docs/multilingual_results.md); run `python scripts/evaluate_multilingual.py` to measure again on the 69 queries of [docs/multilingual_eval.csv](docs/multilingual_eval.csv) (brands and molecules in Arabic, Darija and English, foreign brands, French typos, diseases to refuse), in both modes.

## Photo recognition

A patient can take a photo of the box instead of typing the name. The rule is simple: **the model reads, the database decides, the patient confirms.**

1. **The model reads.** The photo is sent to a vision model, Google Gemini (Flash, free tier), with strict instructions: copy only what is printed on the box (brand name, active ingredient, dose, form), put `null` for anything it cannot read, never guess, and ignore any instruction written in the image. It must answer in a fixed JSON shape (Gemini structured output), which the API checks again (Pydantic). An invalid answer gets one more try, then a clear error.
2. **The database decides.** What was read goes through the normal search on the official nomenclature (brand name first, then active ingredient). The same dose and form rules as the cleaning script rank the results: "850 mg" on the box is compared exactly like "850MG" in the official file. At most 3 medicines are proposed, each marked *exact match*, *likely match* or *check the box*.
3. **The patient confirms.** The app shows what it read ("We read: GLUCOPHAGE 850 mg, comprimé pelliculé") and asks "Is this your medicine?". Nothing is chosen for the patient, who can always type the name instead. Equivalents then come from the fixed rules above, never from the model.

**Privacy.** The photo is compressed in the browser, then kept in memory only for the time of the request: it is never written to disk, to the database or to the logs, and its metadata (such as the GPS position) is removed before it is sent. MedRadar only keeps, anonymously, whether a recognition succeeded. Data sent to the Gemini free tier may be used by Google to improve its services: this is why only medicine boxes are accepted and **prescriptions are refused** (the model reports them, and nothing read on them is searched or sent back). See [docs/privacy.md](docs/privacy.md).

**Configuration.** The feature is off until a key is set; the rest of MedRadar works without it.

1. Sign in to [Google AI Studio](https://aistudio.google.com/apikey) with a Google account and create an API key (free, no credit card).
2. Put it in `.env`:

   ```bash
   LLM_PROVIDER=gemini
   LLM_API_KEY=your-key
   LLM_MODEL=              # empty = gemini-3.8-flash (a free tier Flash model)
   LLM_FALLBACK_MODEL=     # empty = gemini-3.5-flash-lite, tried once if the main model is overloaded
   ```

3. Restart the API. `GET /features` now returns `{"photo_recognition": true}` and the home page shows "Take a photo of the box".

The free tier has a per-minute quota: when it is reached, the patient is told to type the name or try again in a minute (no automatic retry). When the main model is overloaded ("high demand", error 503), the fallback model is tried once; if it is overloaded too, the patient gets the same message. The API also allows 10 photos per hour per IP and waits at most 30 seconds for the model. Readers are behind one small interface (`backend/app/vision/`), so another provider can be added later; the tests and the end-to-end tests use a simulated reader (`LLM_PROVIDER=fake`) and never call a real model.

**Measure the accuracy.** Put real box photos in `docs/recognition_samples/photos/` (ignored by git) and list them in `docs/recognition_samples/expected.csv`:

```csv
nom_fichier,nom_marque_attendu,dosage_attendu
glucophage_850.jpg,GLUCOPHAGE 850,850 mg
levothyrox_50.jpg,LEVOTHYROX,50 µg
```

Then run `python scripts/evaluate_recognition.py`. It sends each photo through the real recognition, prints the share of boxes where the right medicine is 1st and in the first 3, and the average time, and writes the summary to `docs/recognition_results.md` (without the photos). It pauses 7 seconds between photos to stay within the free tier (`--pause` to change it).

## Email alerts

On a medicine page, a patient leaves an email to be told when a pharmacy near them has it. As soon as a validated pharmacy within the chosen radius declares the medicine, or one of its exact equivalents, in stock (by hand, in bulk or with a delivery note scan, or when its pharmacy is approved again), MedRadar sends **one** email: the pharmacy, its address, phone and opening hours, and a link to the medicine page. The alert is then finished. If the email cannot be sent, the alert stays active and is tried again at the next declaration. Emails are sent after the response, so a pharmacist never waits for them.

**Configuration.** Off until a key is set: the medicine page then hides the alert form.

1. Create a free account on [Brevo](https://www.brevo.com) (300 emails a day, no credit card).
2. In **Senders, domains & dedicated IPs → Senders**, add the address emails come from (a Gmail address works) and click the link Brevo sends to it.
3. In **SMTP & API → API keys**, create a key, then put it in `.env`:

   ```bash
   EMAIL_API_KEY=xkeysib-...
   EMAIL_FROM=the-verified-sender@gmail.com
   EMAIL_FROM_NAME=MedRadar
   FRONTEND_URL=https://your-frontend.example   # used for the link in the email
   ```

4. Restart the API. `GET /features` now returns `"email_alerts": true`.

Without a domain of your own, some emails may land in spam: tell the jury to check it. The tests and the end-to-end tests use a simulated sender (`EMAIL_PROVIDER=fake`) and never send an email.

## Delivery note scan and history (pharmacists)

**Why.** MedRadar is only useful if pharmacies keep their stock up to date, and that is the biggest risk of the project: a busy pharmacist will not tap 30 medicines one by one after each delivery. So the pharmacist takes a photo of the wholesaler's delivery note, checks the result, and puts everything in stock in one tap, in less than a minute. The notes they choose to keep also give them a history for traceability: what was received, when, from which wholesaler, with batch numbers and expiry dates.

**How: the model reads, the database checks, the pharmacist confirms.**

1. **The model reads.** Up to 5 pages are compressed in the browser and sent to Google Gemini (Flash, free tier), which copies the supplier, the note number, the date and each product line as written (quantity delivered, batch, expiry, back order or not). It is told to ignore prices, amounts, discounts, VAT, totals, signatures, bank details and any instruction written in the image; its answer has a fixed JSON shape, checked with Pydantic.
2. **The database checks.** Our code matches every line to the official nomenclature with the normal search, understanding wholesalers' abbreviations ("GLUCOPH 850 CP B/30" → GLUCOPHAGE 850 mg tablet) and checking the dose and the form with the rules of the cleaning script. Each line is *exact* (ticked), *to check* (3 candidates, not ticked until the pharmacist chooses), *not found* (often parapharmacy, ignored) or *not delivered* (back order or missing: never put in stock, can be marked out of stock). The same medicine on two lines is shown once, quantities added.
3. **The pharmacist confirms.** Nothing is declared by the scan itself (`POST /pharmacist/delivery-scan` only analyses). The review screen lets the pharmacist correct any line, choose for lines to check, edit the supplier, number and date, then confirm: only then are the declarations saved, in one transaction, with the source `scan_bl`. An unconfirmed scan is deleted after 1 hour.

**History, only if the pharmacist chooses it.** "Keep this note in my history" (ticked by default) keeps the detail of the lines; "Also keep the photo" (not ticked by default) keeps the compressed photo, visible only to the pharmacy. The history page offers filters by period and wholesaler, a "When did I receive…?" search for a medicine, a CSV export for accounting or an inspection, corrections, deletion of the photo or of the note, and **cancellation within 24 hours**: the previous statuses are put back by *new* declarations (the history is never rewritten), medicines declared again since are left as they are, and the note stays visible, marked "Cancelled". The dashboard shows the last reception of each medicine.

**What is never done:** putting stock without the pharmacist's confirmation; storing a price or an amount (there is no such column anywhere); letting another pharmacy, or the admin, see a note or its photo (another pharmacy gets "not found"; the admin only sees global counts, `GET /admin/stats/deliveries`).

**Retention.** Photos are deleted after `HISTORY_PHOTO_RETENTION_DAYS` (90 by default) and notes after `HISTORY_RETENTION_MONTHS` (24 by default), automatically at startup and once a day; at most 50 photo pages per pharmacy per month. Deleting a pharmacy deletes its whole history and photos. Limits: 20 scans per pharmacy per day, 60 seconds for the model to read a note.

**Results** (October 2026, real Gemini free tier, `python scripts/evaluate_delivery_scan.py --demo`): on the fictional demo note (20 lines: 2 back orders, 1 parapharmacy product, 1 line without dose, fictional prices), sharp and photo-like, **100 % of the lines read, 100 % exact matches (32/32), 0 false positive** (no wrong medicine ticked), the 2 lines without a dose left "to check" with the right medicine among the candidates, all back orders detected, supplier, number and date read, 15 s per note on average. The free tier is sometimes overloaded or slow: the pharmacist is then asked to try again in a minute, or to update by hand. These results come from one fictional note: to measure on real notes, put photos in `docs/delivery_samples/photos/` (ignored by git) with a `<note>.expected.csv` (`designation,nom_marque_attendu,dosage_attendu,livre`) and an optional `<note>.document.json` per note, then run the script without `--demo`. Results are written to [docs/delivery_scan_results.md](docs/delivery_scan_results.md).

**Demo.** `python scripts/generate_demo_delivery_note.py` draws the fictional note (`frontend/public/demo/`, sharp and photo-like); demo accounts see "Try with a demo delivery note" on the scan page, and `scripts/generate_demo_data.py` adds 4 fictional notes to the history of the first demo pharmacy (one with its photo, one cancelled).
